Triangles: do an exchange's own three prices ever disagree — for long enough to catch?

Every coin an exchange lists both in dollars and in a bridge currency (BTC, ETH, USDT, on Binance.US and Kraken also USDC, and on Kraken also the euro, which it lists against the dollar) is a triangle: its three prices should agree (X/USD == X/Q × Q/USD). When they don't, three trades in a loop — dollars → coin → bridge → dollars, or the reverse — end with more dollars than they started with, before fees. Nothing leaves the exchange and no second account is involved; the cost is three fees and whatever has moved by the time the orders land. This server watches every one of those markets on the exchange's own feed — Coinbase's full order books, Binance.US's and Kraken's best bid and ask — and re-prices both loops of every triangle each time one of its three markets changes, so the numbers below are what a bot sitting on the same feed would have seen, down to the millisecond. Pick a venue and click Run.

Every loop, best first

"Now" is the loop's gross mismatch at the latest price update. "Median" and "90th" are over samples taken every 20 seconds; "Best" is the largest mismatch seen at any price update, with the dollars the thinnest of the three markets allowed at that moment. "Positive" is the share of recorded time the loop was above zero before fees, and an episode is one unbroken stretch of that. "After latency" is the share of episodes still positive when your first order could have arrived, using the quote that was standing at that moment. "Beat fees" counts episodes where what was left after your latency exceeded the loop's three fees, and "$ / day" is what taking every one of those, on the size available at the peak, would have paid. Click a row to see that loop over time. A big number on a coin with tiny size is a quote on a market nobody trades, not money.
