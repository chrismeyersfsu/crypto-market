Is there money on the table in crypto markets, for someone with a home connection and a small account?

Four ways of asking. Each page is one question, the data this server recorded or fetched to answer it, and the honest answer with costs included. The finding on each card is the short version; the page has the numbers and the controls.

Triangles on one exchange

Do an exchange's own three prices for a coin (X/USD, X/BTC, BTC/USD) ever disagree, for long enough to catch? Live, from the exchange's own feed, re-priced at every tick, for Coinbase, Binance.US and Kraken.

Rarely, and never for enough. The loops that are ever positive run through BTC, ETH and USDT on the big coins, by a fraction of a basis point to about 1.5 bp, on a few hundred dollars, for seconds at a time. A loop costs three fees: 12 to 180 bp on Coinbase depending on tier.

The same coin across exchanges

Is BTC, or a stablecoin, ever cheaper on one exchange than another? Thirty days of minute bars say whether a gap exists; live bid/ask ticks say how many milliseconds it lasts. Plus the BTC/USDT/USD triangle on every venue.

The gap is real but about 1 bp, and only pays at zero fees; every retail fee tier is 20 to 60 times larger. Stablecoins sit at steady offsets between venues, which can be collected once, not repeatedly. The BTC/USDT/USD loop loses money before fees on every venue.

Resting orders on Binance.US

Binance.US charges nothing for an order that sits on the book and is filled by someone else. Twenty strategies replayed against a month of every real trade, asking of each trade whether it would have filled an order sitting there.

Every BTC and ETH strategy lost money, $30 to $1,400 a month on $2,000. A resting order on a moving coin fills only when the price is moving through it, so each fill is on the wrong side of the move by about 4 bp.

Strategy search

A wider net: trend, reversion, one exchange leading another, order flow, pairs, timing, and a basket strategy from a book, about 1,800 variants over a year to five years of candles, with real costs and the last 30% of the data held back.

The main finding is a warning: Binance.US's minute and hour bars are stale, and every spectacular number came from buying a price the next trade corrected. The one thing worth a second look is the basket from the playbook, 30% a year held back against -1% for holding, whose neighbouring settings lose.
