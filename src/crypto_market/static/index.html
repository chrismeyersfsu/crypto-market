Is the same coin ever cheaper on one exchange than another — for long enough to catch?

The one fast, small edge that is actually well-defined: buy BTC where it's cheap, sell it where it's dear, at the same moment. Two datasets, two questions. Thirty days of 1-minute bars from three venues (public) say whether a gap exists and whether a "see it this minute, trade it the next" rule makes money after fees. Live best-bid/best-ask ticks, recorded by this server from each venue's public feed since it last started, say how many milliseconds a gap lasts — the number that decides whether a home connection could ever reach it.

1 bp = 0.01%. Retail fee tiers for a market order: Coinbase ≈ 60 bp, Bitstamp ≈ 40, Kraken ≈ 26, Bitfinex ≈ 20, Binance.US 0 on BTC/USD — but see the "size" column below for what a zero-fee venue's order book actually holds at the best price. Every round trip here pays the fee four times (buy and sell on both venues, then unwind both).

Live ticks: how long does an executable gap last?

A moment is "executable" when selling on one venue and buying on the other beats both fees, right now. An episode is an unbroken run of such moments. "Open after latency" is the share of episodes still there when your order could arrive. "Size at best price" is the median dollars actually on offer at the two prices (the smaller of the two order books' top levels), and "$ per episode" is the whole edge applied to that size — what one perfect, instant execution would have paid. Rows marked steady are a venue's persistent premium: executable almost continuously, but only once — after that round you hold the wrong asset on each side.
